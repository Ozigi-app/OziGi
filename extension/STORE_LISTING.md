## Listing copy

**Name** — Ozigi for LinkedIn

**Short description** (132 char limit, currently 118)

> Find people matching your ideal customer on LinkedIn and send personalised connection requests from your own browser.

**Category** — Workflow & Planning · **Language** — English

**Detailed description**

> Ozigi for LinkedIn turns your ideal-customer profile into real conversations.
>
> Describe who you want to reach in Ozigi. This extension then finds people on LinkedIn who match, and sends each of them a connection request with a note written for that specific person — not a mail-merge.
>
> It works inside your own browser, using your own LinkedIn session, at a human pace: one action every minute or two, with a daily limit you control. Nothing happens while your browser is closed or the extension is switched off.
>
> WHAT IT DOES
> • Finds LinkedIn profiles matching the ideal customer you defined in Ozigi
> • Sends connection requests with a personalised note, under LinkedIn's 300-character limit
> • Skips people you're already connected to, and never sends the same request twice
> • Reports everything back to your Ozigi dashboard, so you can see exactly what was sent and to whom
>
> WHAT IT DOESN'T DO
> • It does not send LinkedIn messages or DMs
> • It does not read your inbox, your messages or your notifications
> • It never sees your LinkedIn password
> • It runs on linkedin.com and nowhere else
>
> Requires a free Ozigi account at https://ozigi.app to generate a connection token.

## Permission justifications

Paste these into the "Privacy practices" tab. `debugger` gets the most scrutiny
of anything here — answer it plainly and don't undersell why it's unavoidable.

**Single purpose**
> Sends LinkedIn connection requests, with a personalised note, to people matching the user's ideal-customer profile, and finds those people via LinkedIn search. Both run in the user's own browser session.

**debugger**
> LinkedIn's interface ignores clicks that were not generated by a real user — its handlers check whether the event came from the browser before opening the invitation dialog. The Chrome DevTools Protocol Input domain is the only way to produce input LinkedIn accepts, so it is the only way this extension can perform the action the user asked for. It is used exclusively to dispatch mouse and key events at coordinates on linkedin.com pages, is attached only while an action runs, and is detached immediately afterwards. No other debugger domain is used: no network interception, no script evaluation, no DOM manipulation through the protocol.

**tabs**
> To open the LinkedIn profile of the lead the user has queued, and to know when that page has finished loading before acting on it.

**scripting**
> The content script reads the LinkedIn page to locate the Connect button and to confirm afterwards that LinkedIn now shows the invitation as pending.

**storage**
> Stores the user's Ozigi connection token, their on/off preference, and the day's action counts, locally on their machine.

**alarms**
> Wakes the extension periodically to check for queued work, and paces actions to a human rate.

**Host permission: linkedin.com**
> Where the connection requests are sent and where the search results are read.

**Host permission: ozigi.app**
> Where the extension receives what to do and reports what it did.

**Are you using remote code?** — No. All code is in the package.

**Data use disclosures** — collects "Personally identifiable information"
(names and public LinkedIn profile URLs of prospects the user searched for).
Certify all three: not sold to third parties; used only for the single purpose
above; not used for creditworthiness or lending.

## Before every submission

- Bump `version` in `manifest.json` (the store rejects a re-used version)
- `npm run pack:extension`, then load the **unpacked zip contents** once and send a
  real connection request — packaging has silently dropped files before
- Confirm no `http://localhost` in `host_permissions` (reviewers flag it, and it
  is not needed: the API sends permissive CORS headers, so local testing still
  works without it)

## Known review risks

- **`debugger` is a red flag permission.** Expect follow-up questions, and expect
  review to take longer than the usual few days. There is no alternative that
  still works — untrusted clicks are silently ignored by LinkedIn.
- **Automation of another service.** The store rejects extensions whose purpose
  violates a third party's terms. LinkedIn's User Agreement restricts automated
  activity, and this sends invitations automatically. That is the single most
  likely reason for rejection, and it is worth deciding how to answer before
  submitting rather than after.
