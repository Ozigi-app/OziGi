## TL;DR

A two-channel sequence works when each channel does the job it is good at. Email carries the message: an intro on day 0, a new-angle follow-up on day 5, and a short breakup on day 12. LinkedIn carries recognition: one personalized connection request on day 2, no pitch attached. You then engage by hand if they accept. Keep daily volume low, let replies on either channel stop everything, and review on day 14. In Ozigi, email and LinkedIn run as two campaigns on the same ICP, so you coordinate the timing yourself. This post gives the full calendar.

We already compared the two channels in [email vs LinkedIn outreach for dev tools](https://ozigi.app/blog/email-vs-linkedin-outreach-dev-tools). That post answers which channel to choose. This one answers what to do when you use both, day by day.

First, a note on what "one sequence" means here, because it changes how you set it up.

## How do email and LinkedIn work together in Ozigi?

They run as separate campaigns. Email is a campaign with steps and delays. LinkedIn is a campaign with one action: a connection request with a personalized note, sent through the [browser extension](https://ozigi.app/docs/linkedin-extension) from your own logged-in tab. They do not share a lead list or interleave into a single queue.

Ozigi does not send LinkedIn messages of any kind. No follow-up after acceptance, no cold DMs. Sequencing on LinkedIn is what gets accounts restricted, so the channel does one thing. Multi-step follow-up belongs on email, where you control the delays and the domain.

So the plan below is a coordination plan. You set up two campaigns on the same ICP, start them on different days, and handle the human parts yourself. One system protects you across both: when a lead replies on either channel, their sequence pauses.

## What does the 14-day calendar look like?

| Day | Channel | Action | Who does it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Email | Intro: one specific observation, one reason for writing, one small ask | Ozigi sends |
| 1 | None | Let it land. Do not touch the lead | |
| 2 | LinkedIn | Connection request with a short note about something different from the email | Extension sends |
| 3 to 4 | LinkedIn | If accepted, react to or comment on one of their posts. No pitch | You, by hand |
| 5 | Email | Follow-up with a new angle: an example, a number, or a short resource | Ozigi sends |
| 6 to 11 | None | Watch for replies. Reply by hand to anyone who answers | You |
| 12 | Email | Breakup: two sentences, easy to answer, no guilt | Ozigi sends |
| 14 | Review | Mark replied leads, move non-responders to a nurture list, check numbers | You |

In the email campaign, set the delays to 5 days after the intro and 7 days after the follow-up. That gives you days 0, 5, and 12.

## Why put the email first and LinkedIn second?

The email carries the information. LinkedIn carries recognition. If the connection request arrives on day 2, the lead has probably seen your name in an inbox. A request from a name they half-remember gets accepted more often than one from a stranger. We have not measured that precisely, so treat it as a hypothesis to test in your own numbers.

Reversing the order works too, but it costs you the pitch. A connection request accepted on day 0 and a cold email on day 2 reads as a sales sequence immediately. Email first keeps the LinkedIn touch low-pressure.

## What should each message say?

Each touch should add something. Repeating the same ask three times is the fastest way to get ignored.

**Day 0 email.** One observation about them that proves you looked: a repo they maintain, a post they wrote. One sentence on why you are writing. One small question. Our guide to [cold email that does not sound like AI](https://ozigi.app/blog/cold-email-that-does-not-sound-like-ai) covers the voice.

**Day 2 connection note.** Reference a different detail from the email. Do not pitch. Do not mention the email unless you have to. Two sentences is plenty. For wording, see [LinkedIn connection message examples](https://ozigi.app/blog/linkedin-connection-message-examples).

**Day 3 to 4 manual touch.** Leave a real comment on a recent post, or react to it. If you cannot write a comment worth reading, a reaction is fine. If the request has not been accepted, do nothing.

**Day 5 email.** A new angle, never a reminder. "Just bumping this" is worse than silence. Send a concrete example, a number from someone similar, or a relevant post. See [how to write a follow-up after no response](https://ozigi.app/blog/follow-up-email-after-no-response).

**Day 12 email.** The breakup. Two sentences. Say you will not write again, and that they can reply if the timing changes. Many replies come here, because the pressure is gone.

## How many touches per day is safe?

Start lower than feels productive. Ozigi defaults to 40 emails and 20 LinkedIn connection requests a day per campaign, and the LinkedIn extension has its own cap across all campaigns. Whichever is lower applies.

For a new domain, 20 to 30 emails a day from a warmed address is a sensible opening, with LinkedIn requests lower still. Ramping slowly costs a few weeks. Getting your domain filtered costs months. Our posts on [how many cold emails per day are safe](https://ozigi.app/blog/how-many-cold-emails-per-day-without-spam) and [warming up a sending domain](https://ozigi.app/blog/how-to-warm-up-sending-domain-2026) have the numbers.

If you plan to send from a new domain, finish the warmup before you start the calendar. A good sequence on a flagged domain still lands in spam.

## What happens when someone replies?

The sequence stops for that lead. Reply detection pauses the lead and marks them as replied, and a reply on either channel pauses them across both. Only that lead stops. The campaign keeps running for everyone else.

This protects the part of the process that costs the most to get wrong: sending your scheduled follow-up the morning after someone answered. Reply by hand, quickly, and in your own words.

Keep your reply habit simple: check replies twice a day during the 14 days and answer within a few hours. A warm reply that sits for two days is a cold one.

## What do you do on day 14?

Review three numbers: replies, positive replies, and bounces. Compare them across the leads you contacted, grouped by ICP score band if you score leads.

- Replied: handle personally. These are your pipeline.
- No reply, accepted connection: keep them on a nurture list. Engage with their posts occasionally.
- No reply, no acceptance: leave them alone for at least 60 days.
- Bounced: check your list quality. A high bounce rate means a sourcing problem, not a copy problem.

If you are sending cold email, keep the legal basics in mind. See [whether cold email is legal under CAN-SPAM and GDPR](https://ozigi.app/blog/is-cold-email-legal-can-spam-gdpr-small-teams) before you start.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long should a cold email and LinkedIn sequence be?**
Fourteen days is long enough for three emails and a connection request without becoming a nuisance. A common shape is an intro on day 0, a follow-up on day 5, and a breakup on day 12, with a LinkedIn connection request on day 2.

**Should I send the cold email or the LinkedIn request first?**
Email first. It carries the actual message, and a connection request two days later arrives from a name the lead has already seen. A LinkedIn request first, followed by a cold email, looks like a sales sequence from the start.

**Does Ozigi send LinkedIn messages?**
No. Ozigi sends one LinkedIn action, a personalized connection request through the browser extension, and no DMs of any kind. Multi-step follow-up runs on email, where you control the delays and your domain reputation.

**How many emails and LinkedIn requests can I send per day?**
Ozigi's defaults are 40 emails and 20 LinkedIn connection requests per campaign per day, with a separate cap in the extension. Start lower: 20 to 30 emails from a warmed domain, and fewer LinkedIn requests. Ramping slowly protects your domain and account.

**What happens if a lead replies to my email?**
Their sequence pauses automatically and their status moves to replied. A reply on either channel pauses them across both. Other leads in the campaign are unaffected.

**What should I do after the 14 days?**
Handle replies yourself, move accepted-but-silent leads to a nurture list, leave non-responders alone for at least 60 days, and check your bounce rate. Use the reply data to tighten your ICP.
